Predicting Sepsis Mortality through Model Tuning and Threshold Analysis with PyTorch

Predicted the risk of death for ICU patients with sepsis using their first 24 hours of data, then lowered the threshold for classifying patients as high-risk to identify more of those who eventually died (55% → 80%).

02Goal

Sepsis is a leading cause of death in intensive care units, making the early identification of high-risk patients critical. This project asked:

  • Can in-hospital mortality be predicted using only data from the first 24 hours?
  • How should the decision threshold be set to minimize missed deaths?
  • Which clinical variables contribute most to the model's predictions?

03Methodology & Summary

  • Data: 911 ICU sepsis patients (270 deaths) with 34 variables, split into 637 for training and 274 for testing.
  • Team scope: The team selected variables with SHAP and VIF, compared 12 machine learning and deep learning models, and chose CatBoost as the best performer.
  • My role: I trained and tuned two deep learning models, TabNet and FT-Transformer.
  • Tuning: Optuna searched model settings and picked the one with the highest average AUC under five-fold cross-validation.
  • Decision threshold: Many of them scored below the default cutoff of 0.5, because only about 30% of patients died. I chose the cutoff that maximized F1 using cross-validated predictions for all 637 training patients.

Test set of 274 patients (74 deaths). AUC is the mean of five seeds; recall, precision and F1 use the cross-validated threshold and the average of the five seeds' predictions.

  • FT-Transformer separated patients better. Its AUC was higher than TabNet's (0.771 vs 0.702), and tuning raised both by about 0.02.
  • The default cutoff missed nearly half of deaths. Lowering the decision threshold increased recall from 55% to 80% at the cost of precision.
  • The two models relied on different variables. TabNet leaned on a severity score (APACHE II); FT-Transformer on level of consciousness (GCS) and a weakened immune system.

05From Prediction to Decision

  • Prioritize first-day monitoring. ICU clinicians could use the risk score to watch high-risk patients more closely from day one.
  • Show the trade-off behind the cutoff. We chose the threshold that maximized the F1 score, balancing the need to identify patients who died against the risk of false alarms, rather than relying on accuracy or the default 0.5 threshold.
  • Limitations: data from one undisclosed source and per-seed thresholds still varied, so results use the five-seed average. Clinical use would need external validation and an agreed alert level.
