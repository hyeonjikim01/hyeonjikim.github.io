Monitoring MDRO Patterns through Time-Based Analysis with SQL and Tableau

Aggregated hospital microbiology test results in accordance with WHO-GLASS surveillance guidelines and built a prototype Tableau dashboard that enables infection control teams to monitor multidrug-resistant organism (MDRO) activity on a single screen.

02Goal

Antimicrobial-resistant infections raise mortality, length of stay and medical costs, so hospitals must continuously monitor and report them. Using microbiology culture results from a university hospital, this project set out to:

  • Count MDRO cases per WHO GLASS guidelines, excluding repeat tests from the same patient.
  • Distinguish hospital-acquired cases from cases acquired outside the hospital.
  • Build a prototype dashboard that allows the infection control team to filter results by organism, specimen type, and time period to monitor MDRO cases at a glance.

03Methodology & Summary

  • Team scope: The team developed an infection surveillance platform with structured lab reports, an alert system, a chatbot, and a dashboard. I was responsible for the dashboard prototype.
  • Data: Microbiology culture results from a university hospital spanning 3.5 years (Jul 2020–Dec 2023), using structured data derived from free-text lab reports by teammates.
  • Deduplication: Wrote a SQL query using window functions to retain only the first test per patient, organism, and specimen within each year, following WHO GLASS deduplication criteria.
  • 48-hour rule: An MDRO detected in a specimen collected more than 48 hours after admission (i.e., more than two calendar days later) was classified as hospital-onset, serving as a proxy for a potentially hospital-acquired infection. Detections within the first 48 hours were classified as community-onset.
  • Dashboard: Built three Tableau tabs (Monitoring, Ward-Level Status, and Statistics) with filters for year, month, organism, and ward.

  • Most cases were detected more than 48 hours after admission. In every month from January to June 2021, MDRO detections after 48 hours outnumbered those within the first 48 hours, highlighting the importance of monitoring potential hospital transmission.
  • Standardized deduplication made case counts more reliable. Counting repeat tests can inflate case counts. Applying WHO-GLASS deduplication criteria produced more consistent counts for surveillance and reporting.

05From Analysis to Decision

  • Spot increases by organism at a glance. KPI cards display each MDRO type’s isolation rate and month-over-month change, helping the team quickly identify rising trends.
  • Monitor potential hospital-acquired cases. The 48-hour indicator helps identify increases in MDRO detections occurring more than 48 hours after admission.
  • Limitations: The dashboard was a prototype and was not deployed. Ward-level data were simulated, the data pipeline was not automated, and no usability testing was conducted with clinicians.
